In the UK, there is no single fixed legal height written into Building Regulations for every wooden mantelpiece. Instead, safe height is set by your stove manufacturer's clearance to combustible materials, your flue type, and sign-off from a HETAS registered installer.

## The short answer: what sets the safe distance?

Three things decide how high a solid oak beam must sit above a wood-burning or multi-fuel stove in the UK, in strict order of priority:

1. **The stove manufacturer's installation manual** (legally takes precedence over general rules of thumb).
2. **UK Building Regulations Approved Document J** (governs clearances around flues and combustible materials where a stove manual does not state a tighter rule).
3. **Your HETAS installer's on-site assessment** (accounts for your chimney breast recess, flue route, stove kW output and beam projection).

| Factor | What to check | Who confirms it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Distance from stove top to oak beam | Manufacturer's stated clearance to combustibles | Stove manual + HETAS installer |
| Distance from single-skin flue pipe | Approved Document J rule for uninsulated flues (typically 3× flue diameter unless shielded) | HETAS installer |
| Distance from twin-wall insulated flue | Flue maker's certified clearance to combustibles | Flue manual + HETAS installer |
| Beam depth (projection into the room) | Deeper beams catch more rising convected heat | HETAS installer before ordering |
| Beam with corbels | Measure from the lowest point of the corbels, not just the beam | Stove manual + HETAS installer |

## 1. Why the stove manufacturer's manual always comes first

Every CE/UKCA-marked wood burner sold in the UK is lab-tested to determine how far heat radiates from its top, sides and rear. Those tested figures are published in the appliance manual as minimum clearances to combustible materials.

Under UK Building Regulations, the manufacturer's instructions override general estimates. Two 5kW stoves can have very different clearance numbers depending on whether they have built-in convector panels, rear heat shields or top vs rear flue exits. Always read the exact manual for your model — never copy a height from a neighbour's fireplace or a social media photo.

## 2. What UK Building Regulations Approved Document J covers

Homeowners often search for an Approved Document J mantel height figure and find conflicting numbers online. Approved Document J does not set one universal height for wooden beams above stoves; it sets legal safety principles around combustion appliances, hearths and flues:

- **Combustible materials near a flue:** Single-skin (uninsulated) flue pipes get extremely hot. Document J sets strict separation from combustible materials (commonly three times the diameter of the flue pipe unless certified heat shielding is fitted).
- **Notifiable work:** Installing a wood-burning stove in England and Wales is notifiable building work. A **HETAS registered installer** self-certifies the installation so your home insurance and future property sale stay protected.
- **Non-combustible construction in the recess:** Inside the fireplace chamber itself, boards, lintels and surrounds must be non-combustible. A solid oak beam sits outside the recess at the safe height confirmed for your setup.

## 3. Why beam depth and heat exposure matter (pyrolysis)

A common mistake is thinking an oak beam is safe simply because it does not scorch on the first night you light the stove.

Long-term exposure to rising heat dries timber out far faster than normal room air. Over months or years, excessive heat can cause **pyrolysis** — a gradual chemical breakdown of wood that deepens splits and lowers the temperature at which the timber can ignite.

That is also why beam depth matters:

- A **shallower beam** (such as a 3" or 4" top depth) projects less into the rising plume of warm air.
- A **chunkier beam** (such as 6" deep) projects further out over the stove and catches more rising heat, so your installer may ask for extra vertical clearance.

## 4. What if your chimney breast does not have enough clearance?

If your HETAS installer confirms you cannot achieve the required distance between the stove and a real oak beam, you have a few practical options:

- **Move the beam higher** if your ceiling height and room proportions allow it.
- **Ask your installer about a heat shield** (some stove makers specify reduced clearances when a compliant heat shield is fitted to the underside of the shelf or rear of the flue — only use what the appliance maker approves).
- **Choose a shallower beam depth** if your installer confirms a smaller projection helps meet the manual's rules.
- **Keep real oak away from the heat zone:** If no compliant position exists for real timber above your fire, do not fit solid wood closer than allowed.

## Questions people ask

### Is there a fixed legal height for an oak mantel beam above a log burner in the UK?

No. UK Building Regulations (Approved Document J) do not give a single universal height for wooden mantel beams. The required clearance is set by your specific stove manufacturer's instructions, the flue pipe clearance rules, and sign-off by a HETAS registered installer.

### Can you put a solid oak mantel beam above a wood-burning stove?

Often yes, provided the beam sits outside the appliance manufacturer's required distance to combustible materials and clear of the flue pipe. Always confirm the exact height with your HETAS installer before fixing the beam.

### Will heat from a log burner crack a solid oak mantel beam?

Oak is a natural timber that expands, contracts and can develop hairline cracks as it settles in a warm living room. At a safe, compliant clearance, normal seasonal movement is expected; if a beam sits too close to rising heat, excessive drying and deep cracking can become a safety risk.

### Can I fit a full solid oak fireplace surround around a log burner?

Only if both the top beam and the two upright legs meet the stove manufacturer's side and top clearances to combustible materials. Check with your installer before ordering a [solid oak fireplace surround](https://www.craftwoodfurniture.co.uk/collections/solid-oak-fireplace-surrounds).

### Do your mantel beams come with fixings?

Yes. Our solid oak mantel beams arrive with pre-drilled mounting holes and concealed fixings suitable for solid walls. For plasterboard, boxed-in chimney breasts or stone, check fixing suitability with your fitter before installation.
